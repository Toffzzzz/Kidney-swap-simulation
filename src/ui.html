Kidney Exchange Simulator

A Monte Carlo model of a kidney paired-exchange scheme. Each run plays out several years day by day: pairs join, matching runs find two-way swaps, three-way swaps and chains, some swaps fall through, and some patients leave. Repeating this many times with different random draws shows the likely result and how much it could vary. The default settings are calibrated to the UK Living Kidney Sharing Scheme.

How the model works

  1. Pairs arrive. Each day new donor–recipient pairs are created at the chosen rate. Each gets donor and recipient blood groups (from that region's frequencies) and a recipient antibody level. Pairs incompatible with each other always join; a share of compatible pairs also join.
  2. Who can give to whom. For each donor and each other patient, the model decides once whether that kidney could go to that patient. Three things must be true: the blood groups are compatible; the crossmatch is negative (a patient with a 95% antibody level reacts to about 95% of donors); and the transplant team would accept the donor on other grounds.
  3. Matching runs. On the chosen timetable, the computer looks for two-way swaps (A↔B), three-way swaps (A→B→C→A) and altruistic chains (donor → pair → pair → waiting list). The hardest-to-match patients go first. Real schemes use exact optimisation; this model uses a fast "hardest first" search, which is close but may find slightly fewer swaps in big pools.
  4. Swaps go ahead or fall through. Each agreed swap may fall through. If it does, the pairs go back into the pool. If it goes ahead, the operation happens after a match-to-surgery delay (at least 45 days, 90 days on average, varying from swap to swap), plus the extra international days if the swap crosses borders.
  5. Pairs leave. Waiting pairs can leave at any time without a transplant.
  6. Measuring. The model runs for 2 years to fill the pool. It then follows everyone who joins over the next 3 years, each for up to 3 more years. All figures describe that group of patients.
  7. Monte Carlo. All of this is repeated many times with different random numbers. The average is the expected result; the 90% range shows how much it could vary.

Calibration against the real UK scheme

The calibrated settings were tuned so that the UK today preset reproduces published NHSBT figures for the UK Living Kidney Sharing Scheme. COVID-affected data is left out: 2020/21, when registrations fell to 91 and altruistic chains to 24, is excluded from pool size and chain numbers. The outcome figures come from April 2021 – March 2025, after the pause. NHSBT's 386-day median wait is not used, because its cohort (registered 2015–2021) waited through the 2020 pause.

The side-by-side comparison with these figures is shown in "Compare with the real UK scheme", under the headline results.

Main limitations

  • Tissue type is not modelled gene by gene. It is summarised by each patient's antibody level and the "donors accepted" setting.
  • The matching search is fast but not exactly optimal, so large pools may slightly understate what an optimiser would achieve.
  • Pool sizes are limited to about 1,500 pairs a year per region so the browser stays responsive. Results level off well before this size.
  • Patients in other regions are assumed to have the same antibody profile and clinical behaviour as UK patients. Only their blood groups differ.
